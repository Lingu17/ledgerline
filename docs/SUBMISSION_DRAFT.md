**Description**

Small companies that bill in several currencies lose hours every week matching incoming payments by hand: missing or messy references, bank fees, short payments, duplicates and wrong currencies. Ledgerline listens for Airwallex `deposit.settled` webhooks and lets rules in code make the first plan for each deposit. When a payment is unclear it asks the payer's own accounts-payable agent over MCP, by itself, as soon as the payment arrives. The answer is a signed claim, accepted only if the signature matches the customer's registered key, the claim is bound to this exact deposit and still fresh, and our own records agree. If there is no agent, Claude reads the remittance email or PDF through four strictly typed tools; its output is advisory evidence, never an instruction. Then Ledgerline acts: settle the invoice, write off a bank fee within tolerance, request the exact balance, hold an advance as credit, convert only the FX shortfall above the reserve, or propose a refund: small refunds backed by a verified answer go out under the policy limit with no person involved, and anything larger needs two people: if the agent proposed it, the person who approves it cannot also send it.

Money rules live in code. Approvals are bound to the exact scope; refunds use stable request ids, a per-deposit duplicate lock and treat SENT as not final (only PAID releases the liability); a pause switch stops all money-out. Every currency reconciles to the cent: deposits = applied + credits + refunds + unapplied.

It is a working multi-tenant SaaS: sign-up (email or Google), organizations with owner/operator/viewer roles, four-eyes approvals, CSV import, audit export, isolated data per organization. In the reference scenario 4 of 6 deposits are decided without a person (3 fully closed; a part payment still needs someone to send the balance request), one refund needs an approval, one bad agent claim is escalated with its reason, and there are 0 double payments; a randomized harness checked the rules, with automatic asking switched off, in 51,475 checks across 200 synthetic companies and 25 sample walkthroughs with 0 mismatches.

**Honest scope**

- Runs on one Google Cloud VM (SQLite per organization, nightly backups on the same disk), Free Trial credit until 28 Jan 2027.
- Any company can connect its own Airwallex sandbox keys (checked first, encrypted per organization, test environment only). Practice companies use a built-in simulated bank. Payer agents are simulations on the server: real MCP and Ed25519, but answers come from a fixed table and the keys sit in the same deployment, so the signature proves the mechanism rather than an independent party. A customer's own hosted agent cannot be connected yet. Where there is no agent, rules, the document reader and a person handle the case. A practice company pauses before asking so a viewer can watch; real companies do not. Automatic asking is tested through a webhook on the simulated bank; no customer on a connected account has an agent registered, so it has not run on one.
- Short-cash decisions: bills due soon are ranked by priority and date, then funded, converted, deferred or escalated; new cash reopens only the decisions it changes. Deciding moves no money. A funded bill can then be paid through the bank: a second person approves the exact amount and destination, the plan is rechecked before sending, and the bill closes only on PAID. Cash can be set aside from planning, so a pre-funded sandbox wallet can still show a real shortfall. To close a gap it can also bring cash in: a discount capped by policy on an invoice not yet due, offered through a hosted Airwallex link. The discount and cash are booked only when Airwallex reports the link paid, and the bills are then re-planned. Every plan change stores its inputs and can be replayed to the identical answer, and the audit log is hash-chained: editing or removing an event is detected. Rewriting the whole log with database access is caught only by comparing the head hash with a saved copy.
- Verified against the Airwallex sandbox: signed deposit webhooks, deposits, an EUR→GBP conversion with single-use quotes, a refund through SENT→PAID (proposed and approved with two of the owner's accounts), current rates, beneficiary schema and validation, deposit listing as a webhook fallback. Hosted payment links: on 2 Nov a Singapore sandbox account created an early-payment link, which was paid by test card, reported paid and booked once. A EUR 100 bill payment on the same account went through SENT to PAID by sandbox simulation; its proposer and approver were two accounts of the same owner, so it tests the control and the bank call, not independent review. An earlier account was refused for its region, and there Ledgerline keeps a labelled draft instead of inventing a URL. Airwallex's dashboard replay did not produce a second delivery, so replay protection is shown by tests.
- A payment in the wrong currency is held, and rules in code recommend converting only what the invoice needs or holding it when a bill in that currency is short. A person clicks to convert; it never happens by itself. Claude can propose a conversion through a typed tool but cannot state an amount. Tested on the simulated bank, using the same quote and conversion calls verified live for bills.
- Balance requests come with a ready email draft and the exact-balance link. A person sends it from their own mail; Ledgerline has no email delivery.
- A CEO view answers on one screen whether the company is safe, what the agent handled, what needs a person and whether the numbers can be trusted. Bills can name the invoice their payment releases: within a priority, one that releases more than it costs goes first; this ordering is tested, not used by a real customer.
- Connected accounts are checked in the background every two minutes: paid links are booked and missed deposits are found, so nobody has to remember to click.
- Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`) was exercised live and returned a schema-valid proposal stored as advisory evidence.
